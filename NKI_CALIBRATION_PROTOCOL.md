# Does a personal baseline scan reduce target calibration?

Recorded after the NKI frozen-decoder experiment and before scoring the calibration curves. The practical comparison is between a decoder from the target person's baseline scan, a pooled decoder trained on the other 24 people, and a decoder fitted using only target calibration data. Every method receives the same labeled target time steps and predicts the same new test sequences. This measures delayed recall of simulated inputs on measured wiring.

## Starting decoders

Use the 25 paired NKI scans, fixed atlas inputs, radius 0.90, tanh reservoir, and ten delays from the repeat-scan experiment. The personal starting weights come from the baseline scan and use the transfer penalty selected on other participants' repeat-scan validation signals, excluding the target person. The pooled starting weights and intercept come from the original budget-matched, leave-one-person-out fit. The target-only method starts with zero weights and intercept, the input distribution's known mean. At zero calibration keep each starting model intact. Verify both learned starting models against their published local repeat-scan scores on the original test streams. Use fresh streams to score this experiment.

## Target calibration budget

Test 0, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 and 2,352 labeled simulated time steps. Each time step supplies targets for ten delays. Each positive budget takes equal-length prefixes from four independently generated calibration sequences following a 200-step warmup. Prefixes are nested across budgets. Repeat with six independently seeded sets of four calibration sequences. Test all fits for a participant and visit on the same four fresh 512-step sequences, disjoint from every calibration draw and the earlier study. Odd diffusion directions with baseline as source and the other visit as target are primary. Evaluate even directions and both reverse comparisons as sensitivity analyses on the same 25 people.

At each positive budget fit a linear correction around the starting decoder using ridge regression. For finite penalties estimate the intercept from the calibration samples, leave it unpenalized, and penalize the change in slope coefficients. Use the same input-feature and output units for all starting models. Select a relative penalty from powers of ten between 10⁻¹⁴ and 10⁴, also allowing the original decoder to remain untouched. Select by squared prediction error in four-fold cross-validation, holding out one entire calibration sequence per fold. Refit using all budgeted samples. No extra target labels, target validation sequences or test signals enter calibration or penalty selection. Using blocks of independently simulated sequences prevents adjacent or overlapping delayed targets from appearing on opposite sides of an inner fold.

## Decisions and uncertainty

The primary sample-saving outcome is the first tested budget at which mean held-out R² reaches 0.50 and remains there at every larger budget. Assess the cohort curve and the participant curves, averaging repeats before determining crossings. Report fractions reaching the threshold, paired earlier, equal and later crossing counts, and any nonattainment. Show 0.80 and 0.90 as secondary thresholds. Crossings are resolved only on the tested grid.

The primary accuracy contrast is personal minus pooled R², averaged over the six budgets from 8 through 256, weighted equally. Use one contrast per person after averaging the six repeats. An average difference of 0.01 R² in either direction is the chosen practical reference. Summarize conditional uncertainty with 10,000 paired participant resamples. A narrow interval supports a conclusion only for the tested procedure, source models, task and cohort. Show comparisons with target-only training, the full accuracy curves, and repeat-visit-direction sensitivities. For an individual budget, report small differences with sufficient precision to distinguish them from rounding.

If both starting models first sustain R² of 0.50 by 128 examples on the primary cohort curve, refine their crossing brackets at four-time-step intervals, the smallest equal increment across the four sequences. Use the union of the two brackets, identical seeds, and the original test sets. Preserve the original grid and endpoints and report these refined crossings as a secondary estimate of sample savings.
