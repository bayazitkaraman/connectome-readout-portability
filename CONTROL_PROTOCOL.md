# First controlled comparison of shared-decoder transfer
Specified after the Lausanne-only pilot and before scoring surrogates. Reuse its participant folds, input map 10, gain 0.90, fresh train/validation/test sequences, sample budgets, 10 delays and expanded ridge grid. The existing expanded-grid human comparison is the reference. The same random inputs in real and surrogate simulations make this a paired comparison. No retuning using final test performance.

## Null construction
Use 24 independent draws of each family. Each draw applies a common permutation of upper-triangle edge positions to all people in a fold. This keeps each person's full edge-weight distribution and density and the 70-by-70 interperson edge-vector correlations; the signed input map and input-to-input / input-to-output / output-to-output weight totals (split by input sign) are preserved. Train a new shared readout and new individual reference readouts for every surrogate.

The first family exchanges profiles within signed endpoint roles only. In the second family, constrain exchanges also by mean edge weight (three positive quantile bins and a zero bin), edge prevalence (three positive quantile bins and a zero bin), and the two endpoints' average node-strength terciles. Estimate these bins on only the 42 inner-training people of each fold and apply to the full fold. If independent atlas ordering is verified before scoring a spatial extension, run a third family additionally constraining exchanges by hemisphere pair and template Euclidean distance quartiles. Do not infer geometry from unlabeled matrix order; unverified spatial matches are reported as open. Count actual changes and check regional degree and strength shifts on held-out people.

## Estimand and adequacy checks
Primary comparison: surrogate minus real 10-delay mean transfer cost in normalized MSE (shared minus individual). Positive means the shared decoder is relatively easier to transfer on human wiring. Also show offered absolute shared and individual R², their difference, lag profiles and dispersion across draws. The difference in costs can arise solely from different achievable individual accuracy, so explicitly check it before interpretation. Report the between-cohort mean individual R² difference and root-mean-square of the ten mean delay-specific individual differences. Use 0.03 and 0.05 R² points, respectively, as practical descriptive match checks. For regional degree and strength report sums of absolute node-level differences relative to the corresponding participant total, averaged across people. A mean shift above 20% on either counts as a material remaining mismatch. These are exploratory tolerances. A close task match or small contrast alone does not establish formal equivalence. Template Euclidean distance is not measured tract length. Repeat scans or a new cohort are needed for individual or general-population claims.

## Conditional follow-up

The first two control families exceeded the regional-strength tolerance. Before inspecting the spatial scores, we specified a fourth, exploratory sensitivity analysis if the spatial control also exceeded either regional tolerance. Shuffle the edges within each spatial stratum into disjoint pairs, then swap each pair with probability one half. Repeat this procedure 24 times using the same folds, scoring and comparative checks. Report both full and partial spatial results and quantify the magnitude of the perturbation. The partial perturbation leaves more of the original wiring in place, limiting what similar decoder losses would imply.

After the partial control also exceeded the regional-strength tolerance, specify one final control that uses training wiring to decide which swaps to accept. Propose disjoint random edge pairs within the same spatial strata in random order. Accept each swap if the resulting relative L1 shifts of regional strength and degree each average at most 15% in the 42 inner-training people. The 15% training cutoff leaves a margin below the existing 20% held-out tolerance. Apply the resulting permutation to the test graphs without modification. Score 24 draws once, show all previous controls, and quantify held-out shifts, recall and weight movement. Treat a movement below 10% of total weight as a weak perturbation even if matching passes. This control was developed on the same cohort and needs independent confirmation.
