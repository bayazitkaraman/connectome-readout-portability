# NKI split-half diagnostic for zero-label decoder portability

Exploratory follow-up motivated by the earlier single-donor, augmented-pool and density results. The question is how much transfer the same person's wiring supports between two reconstructions of one acquisition, relative to two different visits. Prior cross-visit results are already known.

**Primary conditions.** Use all 25 NKI participants. For each visit, train on odd diffusion directions and test on even diffusion directions from the same acquisition. Compare with training on odd directions from the other visit and testing on the **same target even graph and identical test signals**. Repeat with even and odd exchanged. Compute all 12 ordered source-to-target combinations among the four distinct half/visit cells, including the original odd first-to-odd second benchmark. Treat the two visits as repeated measurements on the same 25 people. Report visits separately.

**Frozen transfer.** Use the locked conn2res map and gain, ten delays and per-person ridge model. Each source first receives 2,352 simulated fitting steps. For a target and each of its 24 comparison people, choose a common ridge penalty on the remaining 23 identities' transfer-validation signals. Score the two frozen source decoders on the same four 512-step target test streams, with zero target calibration labels. Use streams keyed by visit and participant, with disjoint fit, parameter-validation and scored streams. As a sensitivity check, apply the cross-visit transfer penalty to the within-visit comparison too. Treat direct target-graph fitting separately and report its labels.

**Structural identifiability and task transfer.** Compare the target with all 25 source graphs using Pearson correlation of off-diagonal upper-triangle weights including zeros. Report own and mean-stranger correlations, own above the mean stranger, strict top-one retrieval and ties. For task scores, report own and average stranger R², their paired difference, how many targets favor their own decoder, and the between-participant association between anatomical and accuracy advantages. Average over the 24 comparisons per person. Use a paired participant bootstrap with 10,000 draws conditional on graph fits and the observed source bank, including the paired own-score difference for same- versus cross-visit transfer to each identical target. Keep all participants, including structurally unusual pairs. Check the released matrices and session records before drawing conclusions about an individual pairing. A shared acquisition and shared processing make this a within-acquisition comparator and do not give a mathematical upper bound on cross-visit accuracy.
