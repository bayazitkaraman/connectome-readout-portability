# Input placement and graph sensitivity of repeat-scan portability

This follow-up specification was written after the original paired outcomes and the earlier donor-selection sensitivities were known, before scoring the new matched-noise variants.

## Participants, targets and fixed choices

Use all 25 NKI participants (first-visit odd directions to second-visit odd directions) and all 57 Beijing BNU1 participants (whole first visit to whole second visit). Each evaluated pair belongs to the same participant. The endpoint is the equal-weight mean held-out R² of ten linear delayed-recall outputs. Use the original source training, target validation and target test stream identifiers and budgets in every variant: 2,352 training steps, 1,176 independent validation steps, and four fresh 512-step test streams after 200 warm-up steps per stream. Within each test pair drive the two graphs with identical inputs. Only the identity of driven regions or the graph setting will change; input signals do not change across variants.

## Settings

1. On the native 83-region weighted graph at radius 0.90, use ten signed input maps drawn by the existing deterministic mapping function with consecutive keys 10 through 19. Key 10 is the original. Each map drives eight distinct nodes with four positive and four negative weights (magnitudes 0.5) and reads out the other 75. Report every map, including any poorly performing map. Keys were fixed independently of outcomes.
2. On map 10, change one factor at a time: native graph at spectral radius 0.70 or 1.00; log(1+weight), binary edges, and the strongest fixed number of weighted edges (177 NKI, 1,291 Beijing, established tie seed 61973) at radius 0.90. Remove the diagonal before scaling as in the original analysis. The original native 0.90 case belongs to both comparisons and is scored only once. The design therefore uses 15 settings per cohort. No parcellation, task, hardware or participant subgroup sweep is included.

## Independent selection and scoring

For each source person and setting fit the source-centered ridge readout on the original training stream. Use the original 19 relative penalties from 10⁻¹⁴ through 10⁴. For each participant calculate the cross-visit mean validation squared prediction error over ten outputs on that person's second graph for each penalty. When choosing a penalty for an evaluated person's pair, exclude that person and minimize the mean of the remaining participants' validation losses. There is no comparison stranger in this experiment. The evaluated person's repeat labels are used only to score its result. Ties use the first, least regularized grid value. Training covariance scaling is recomputed for each source and setting. An entirely constant source design uses an intercept-only readout and has undefined relative displacement when its training fluctuation is zero; retain and count these cases.

For sensitivity report the same evaluation with the original cohort transfer penalty fixed (10 NKI, 1 Beijing). Also score α = 0.001 to check the light-regularization regime. Score the very same frozen weights on the source, on its repeat, and in expectation under independent isotropic observation noise on the source matched to this pair’s measured squared state displacement. The primary difference is expected matched-noise R² minus measured repeat R²; positive means the noise overestimates portability. Record the matched-noise loss, the normalized squared projection of the measured displacement through the readout, and its normalized cross term with the source residual. Verify that their sum/difference reconstructs the measured excess. Compare fixed-penalty native-map repeat scores to the existing pipeline sensitivity output where available and verify the full primary native map against the archived participant-level shift analysis.

## Reporting and uncertainty

Report source, expected noise and measured repeat R², excess loss, source-training-normalized state displacement, counts of positive excess and positive repeat scores, and the selected penalty distribution. Use paired target-person percentile bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws, seed 20261120) conditional on observed maps, donor validation results and simulated signals. Also average the nine new native maps within each participant before producing a pooled summary; use the same participant draws jointly across maps. Descriptive between-map ranges are ranges over a finite prespecified set. These maps and reconstructed graphs are repeated conditions, not extra participants. Report uncertainty, reversals and near-zero performance rather than enforcing a positivity cutoff or selecting favorable maps.
