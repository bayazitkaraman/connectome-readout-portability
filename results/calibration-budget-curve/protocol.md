# Equal target-state budgets for unlabeled alignment and labeled correction

This follow-up specification precedes scoring the budget curve. The original frozen results and the 2,352-state unlabeled alignment results were known. The question is how parcelwise alignment behaves at smaller budgets and how much labeling those same states adds under an explicitly defined correction rule.

Use all 25 NKI and 57 Beijing native first-to-second, own-person pairs, the original eight signed inputs and radius 0.90, ten single-input delays, 2,352 labeled source rows, and the archived source transfer penalties 10 (NKI) and 1 (Beijing). Reuse four independent role-61 test streams, each with 512 scored steps and 200 warmup steps. Three strategies see the same test targets.

1. Use the original frozen first-scan decoder without adaptation.
2. Apply the existing parcelwise target-to-source affine transformation from unlabeled target states, then apply the frozen decoder. Use the source training mean and population standard deviation and the target calibration mean and population standard deviation. A parcel constant in either reference series maps to the source mean.
3. Use the same alignment and frozen prediction, then fit a linear residual correction from the original target state to each of the ten labeled errors on exactly those same calibration rows. The residual intercept is unpenalized. Original target features allow the correction to use a measured target parcel even when its source series was constant. Candidate penalties multiply the mean target calibration variance across parcels in a centered, covariance-form ridge fit. The 19 candidates are powers of ten from 10⁻¹⁴ to 10⁴; an additional no-correction candidate sets both residual slopes and intercept to zero, exactly restoring the unlabeled strategy. At budget zero all three strategies give the unadapted frozen prediction.

Positive target budgets are 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024 and 2,352 rows. Draw the same full role-219 target stream as the original alignment run, discard its 200-step warmup, and use nested prefixes. In this simulation an input's history yields the ten delay labels. Withholding labels is an evaluation rule. No claim about the real cost of acquiring human labels follows from this comparison.

Select the correction penalty separately for each cohort, budget and held-out participant. Each other participant supplies a calibration prefix from their own role-219 stream; score all their candidate corrections by the mean squared error averaged across all ten outputs on their own independent role-1 target validation stream (1,176 scored rows). Average validation MSE equally across these other participants. Choose minimum MSE, with the strongest penalty (no correction first) preferred at an exact tie within 10⁻¹² MSE. The evaluated person's role-1 labels have no say in this choice; no role-61 scores enter selection. At application time, no extra labeled target rows beyond the stated budget are consumed. This rule permits the labeled method to decline a correction if it performs poorly on the external validation panel.

Score ordinary held-out R² separately for each delay and average equally across ten delays, then across participants. Report cohort means, participant medians and counts, the paired changes aligned minus frozen and labeled minus aligned, plus labeled minus frozen. Use 10,000 paired percentile bootstrap draws of participants (seed 20261120), reusing the draw indices across arms and budgets; intervals are pointwise and conditional on the observed tuning panel, fitted readouts and single nested calibration stream. Fractions of source R² use a ratio of cohort means. Do not select a minimum effective sample size according to statistical significance.

Verify all 82 baseline and full-budget alignment results against the archived participant table; use direct independent predictions to check the residual solver and validation loss, and explicitly check that the held-out participant is absent from penalty selection. Produce one compact study figure, the participant-level results and candidate validation errors, group summaries, a short interpretation and a machine-readable check record.
