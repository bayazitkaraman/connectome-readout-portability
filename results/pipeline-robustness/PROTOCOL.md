# Zero-label pipeline sensitivity

Use the original linear delayed-recall task and visit 1 to visit 2. Each condition uses every directed source–target pair for all 25 NKI or 57 Beijing participants. Retain the original input map, streams, train/test sizes, and fixed native cross-visit ridge penalty (10 in NKI, 1 in Beijing). Recompute source models and outcome-blind donor choices after changing a graph. Report mean other, closest other, personal R², paired closest-minus-mean-other interval, top-one wiring retrieval, and mean R² by source–target wiring similarity for own and stranger pairs separately. The participant bootstrap holds the donor bank and simulated inputs fixed. Check native baseline scores against the archive.

One change at a time: replace positive weights with log(1+w), replace them with one, retain exactly the strongest 177 (NKI) or 1,291 (Beijing) edges with the prior fixed tie seed 61973, use reservoir spectral radius 0.70 or 1.00 in place of 0.90, or use ten times or one tenth the native ridge. For a transformed graph its correlation and reservoir both use the transformed weights. Compare curves within their observed support. Similarity bins are the previously reported boundaries 0.45, 0.60, 0.75, 0.85, and 0.92. No clinical or universal threshold is prespecified.

Scale sensitivity uses the released Schaefer 200- and 300-parcel networks from the same 57 Beijing participants at both visits. Eight cortical input sites are selected at 200 parcels with the same map seed. Match them one-to-one to the 300-parcel atlas by hemisphere and nearest physical centroid under minimum total Euclidean distance. Signs and test inputs stay fixed. All other nodes are readouts at each resolution. Use fixed ridge 1 and radius 0.90. Only the two Schaefer conditions form the within-family scale comparison. Resolution changes the state dimension and parcel boundaries and the map is an anatomical approximation. Lausanne's single-session individual release is a separate dataset and cannot establish a repeat-scan resolution effect.
