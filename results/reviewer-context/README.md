# Cohort context and paired sensitivity checks

Run `python scripts/reviewer_context.py --nki-age-index /path/to/aws_links.csv` from the benchmark root after preparing the two cohorts and generating the study results. The optional argument reproduces the aggregate age table from the [Rockland imaging index](https://rocklandsample.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/aws_links.csv). The script verifies its SHA-256 and checks the baseline diffusion ages against the T1-weighted records. The participant-level age records are kept at the original provider.

The graph counts refer to undirected, nonzero, off-diagonal edges among the 83 native regions. Identification compares the second-visit scan with the entire first-visit bank, including the person's own graph. Donor matching instead excludes the person's own graph. It compares each target's closest stranger against a target-blind policy that favors donors often selected for other people. Both roles of the evaluated participant are excluded when estimating those selection frequencies. The additional restricted-bank sensitivity disallows the two most frequently chosen NKI donors and all three Beijing donors tied for first. Their exclusion applies only to the donor role. The target sample remains intact. Percentile intervals use 10,000 target-level bootstrap draws conditional on the available donor bank.

The numerical audit uses the deterministic, first-visit, odd-direction graphs. Twenty perturbed runs per person yielded 2–10 distinct off-diagonal graphs (median 4). The reference was recovered in 246 of the 500 runs. All 25 participants had at least one changed graph. At relative penalty 0.001, 23 participants had a positive mean over all 20 runs, compared with 20 when the mean included only changed runs. Repeated copies of a changed graph retain their observed frequencies.

For the spatial decomposition, let D be the target states minus the source states on identical input, T the number of test time points, and w the frozen weights for one output. Define G = DᵀD/T, with no column centering. A zero-mean perturbation independent of the signal and source residuals with row covariance G adds expected mean squared error wᵀGw. One exact finite-sample construction independently draws a row of D uniformly, with replacement, and multiplies it by an independent, equally probable sign at each test time. The construction has covariance G and breaks the time alignment with the source residual. The measured repeat also contributes the cross term 2eᵀDw/T, where e is the source prediction minus the true output. The isotropic control instead uses covariance tr(G)I/p for p readout regions. Dividing each output's loss by its own target variance and averaging outputs yields the reported loss in R². The spatial expectation is an analytic counterfactual conditional on the observed displacement. It does not require a new simulation or estimate a cause of the perturbation.

The fraction attributed to the second-moment match is the ratio of cohort mean differences, (spatial minus isotropic)/(measured minus isotropic). The paired participant bootstrap recomputes both means and their ratio in each draw. Under this definition, the fractions were 0.532 in NKI and 0.833 in Beijing. The remaining excess is the residual cross term. The direction-half augmentation table reports the paired first-visit training effect and the mean after removing its single largest observed gain. The flagged-participant donor checks, including exclusion from tuning, are in `results/transfer-similarity/flagged_sensitivity.json`.
