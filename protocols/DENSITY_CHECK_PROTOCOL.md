# Checks on the proposed density explanation

This exploratory analysis follows the reported eight-example anatomy association. Both cohorts have already supplied results. Use the original first-to-second scan direction and the original primary equal-donor allocation. Report the fixed 25% allocation as a sensitivity. Compare anatomical similarity and recall gains within cohort.

1. Count unique positive undirected edges after removing the diagonal and describe the two visits separately. Evaluate scan-rescan density agreement. Use the original matched pairs to relate own-minus-other recall R² to own-minus-other raw-weight correlation and density mismatch advantage, defined as the other first scan's absolute density difference from the target second scan minus the personal first scan's absolute density difference. Compare pair-level crossed target/comparison-person models with participant-average partial rank correlations. Check binary support overlap (Jaccard) and correlation of positive weights on the shared support. Report model conditioning and uncertainty.
2. Probe state amplitude using the identical simulated source input (stream 29000, role 80) on both visits of all subjects. Measure RMS amplitude across all non-input readout regions over 2,352 steps following the usual warmup; include isolated regions. Check per-visit density versus amplitude and person-averaged density-mismatch versus log-amplitude-mismatch advantage. Check the latter's association with eight-example accuracy gain. This directly probes one aggregate scale hypothesis. It does not measure mediation or all regional changes in activity.
3. Check whether the observed correlation depends entirely on sharing the same comparison person in the two contrasts. For each of the three preassigned comparator offsets, calculate a participant-level rank correlation using that offset for anatomy and the other two offsets for accuracy, then average the three correlations. Compare the original measure with uncertainty from resampling people and, within sampled people, their three observed comparisons. Report intervals conditional on these observed donor choices and fixed training fits.
4. Report the six matched positive budgets and the zero-example condition. Compute average early gain as the trapezoidal integral of the own-minus-other R² difference over 0, 8, 16 and 32 labels divided by 32. Preserve the previously specified R² threshold for sample savings. Compare slopes across cohorts and assess the limited common anatomical range. Interpret correlations at near-zero between-person variation with care.
5. Evaluate a separate perturbation with each scan retaining the same number of strongest positive undirected edges within its cohort. Use the smallest observed edge count in that cohort and fixed tie priorities shared across people. Rerun the paired scan comparison and check target-network performance. Fixed density removes edge-count variation while also pruning connections. A collapse or persistence after pruning does not identify density as a sole cause.

The efficacy of a density-based donor-selection rule, effects of age, the source of scanner or tractography variation and false-negative rates for synthetic identity signals are distinct questions. Distinguish measurements from hypotheses and mark unperformed tests as such.
